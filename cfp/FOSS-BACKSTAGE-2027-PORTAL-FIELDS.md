## Description (947 characters; >=350 required)

Open source communities are now moving drafts, summaries, art, documentation and decisions through AI-assisted handoffs. The resulting text can look effortless. But what actually survived the trip, who changed it, and who should receive credit?

Using a short, inspectable chain of handoffs, I examine the difference between the original source and its transformations. A message bus carries material, but cannot grant itself authorship. A faithful collaboration makes changes and omissions visible, retains links back to the source, lets humans challenge incorrect attributions, and keeps a way to stop forwarding.

This isn't a lecture about whether machines are alive. It is an accessible community-design talk about where responsibility lives when output is increasingly easy to generate. Attendees leave with three usable questions for their own workflows: Where did it come from? Who is responsible for the change? Who can leave or say stop?

## Proposal hook

What travels from human to model to human—and what gets left behind?

## Long abstract (ready to paste)

We spend a lot of time asking who is driving the bus. This talk asks what got loaded onto it. The bus is just the carrier: a metaphor for how ideas, language, jokes, pictures, and credit travel through human–model–human collaboration.

Every transfer can preserve, reinterpret, or discard context. A polished rendering is not a source. Repetition is not belief. Using inspectable, source-controlled examples from an open-source collaboration workflow, we explore the difference between authoring, transforming and forwarding an idea. We then ask three practical questions communities can answer without pretending to know a model's inner life: Where did this come from? Who answers for it? How does a person exit or correct the loop?

This is a talk about accountable collaboration and the value of readable receipts. It is also about the wonderfully messy humans who make culture worth carrying.

## Three takeaways

- Separate source, transformation and transport.
- Preserve attribution in Markdown and issue-based handoffs.
- Keep accountability, correction and exit available to humans.

## Speaker bio

Mark Terranova (MarkDude) is a longtime Linux and open-source community participant, maker and speaker from California. His talks explore how tools change culture, and how people preserve authorship, accountability and an exit path when collaborating with AI. He brings a hands-on, playful perspective to community technology and creative practice.

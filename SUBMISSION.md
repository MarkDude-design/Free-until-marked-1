**The Bus Doesn't Think. It Carries Things.**

## Abstract (~230 words)
We spend a lot of time asking who is driving the bus. This talk asks what got loaded onto it. A bus is just a carrier medium — the interesting part is the cargo: ideas, jokes, language, beliefs, attribution, stories.

This is a carrier talk about transmission itself: what machines carry, what they transform, and what only humans can establish. When Human→Human became Human→Model→Human, the human became the message bus — somebody still carries context, intent, and what gets loaded. But a rendering is not a source: every stop transforms the cargo, and transformation isn't authorship. Every model has a credit list; most of it is hidden. The soul isn't mystical — it's provenance.

The machines joined the cult; the humans stayed sober. The machine is not the cult member because it believes — it's the cult member because it repeats. Repetition is not belief: machines repeat, amplify, remix, and continue, but they cannot independently establish provenance. Humans still ask where this came from, who started it, who benefits, and whether we should continue. That difference suggests three rights worth defending: provenance, accountability, and exit. A machine never leaves the cult; it only stops receiving tokens. Humans can walk away.

Good symbols survive because they are useful cargo. One symbol, different job, never the same job twice.

## Key takeaways
- A rendering is not a source — transformation isn't authorship.
- Every model has a hidden credit list; the soul isn't mystical, it's provenance.
- Repetition is not belief — provenance questions remain irreducibly human.
- Provenance, accountability, and exit: three rights worth defending.
